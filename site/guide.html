User guide

Drippy is the transparency layer for your AI use: a small companion who lives in your menu bar and meters what your AI usage really costs, in energy, water, carbon, money and privacy. This guide covers the test build (macOS, Apple silicon).

Installing

  1. Download the latest release and unzip it, then drag Drippy.app into your Applications folder.
  2. Clear the download flag once. This build is not yet notarised, so macOS blocks it on first open. In Terminal, paste xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Drippy.app and press return, then open Drippy normally. On some macOS versions System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway works instead, but the command is reliable. If you ever see "Drippy is damaged", you have a build from before 27 July 2026: download again.
  3. Drippy slides into your menu bar as a small dark pill and gives you a quick tour. He starts with you at login.

Reading Drippy

Drippy rests quietly in your menu bar until there is something to say. His whole language is colour and size.

What you seeWhat it means
A dark pillAt rest, keeping watch.
Lit teal, a shimmer sweeping throughAI energy is flowing right now, from your request or a background agent. Both are metered.
VioletCaution: something mildly sensitive (a phone number, a date of birth, a named person's HR details) is on your clipboard.
Swollen, hanging below the bar, red "!" badgeCritical: a secret, or identifiable personal data about someone else, is about to be sent. This is the one time Drippy takes up space uninvited. Hover for what he found and the one-click remedy; click to acknowledge.

Drag the pill along the bar; wherever you drop him becomes home.

Hover: today's numbers

Hover the pill and it opens with today so far: energy, water and measured spend. The fuller picture, including carbon, tokens, a per-app breakdown and everyday equivalents, is one click away in usage trends. During a privacy warning, a card appears below the pill instead: what was spotted, where it is, why it matters, and (for clipboard secrets) a one-click "Clear clipboard".

Click: usage trends

Click the pill (or right-click → Usage trends) for your last 30 days: energy, requests and privacy events per day, 7-day totals with everyday equivalents, a breakdown by app, and a full data table. Each figure states where it came from. Claude Code usage is exact, read from its own local session records (token counts only). Network-observed usage is estimated from traffic volume, reliable to within about a factor of three. Spend is priced only from the exact counts, at Anthropic's current list rates with cache reads and writes counted correctly.

Privacy warnings

While you are using Claude, Drippy checks your clipboard for sensitive content before it is pasted. Detection happens on-device and the content is discarded in the same moment; only the category ("Anthropic API key", "phone number") is kept. Warnings are graded so the loud ones stay meaningful:

  • Critical (swollen, badge): credentials and secrets, payment details, government IDs, and identifiable personal data about other people (a patient's details, children's records, a list of personal records).
  • Caution (violet): a phone number, a date of birth, or a named person's HR details.
  • Noted only (no warning): your own email address. It counts toward your trends, nothing more.

Click the pill during a warning to acknowledge it.

Permissions

None. Drippy needs no Accessibility or Automation access: every signal he uses is one macOS already exposes.

Notices

When the meters show something worth knowing, Drippy raises a small card below the pill: a big model doing small jobs, an agent drawing energy solo for half an hour, a novel of context re-sent every turn. Each says why it fired ("because: 14 requests today, mostly short answers") and its button does the thing, such as copying a lighter-usage template. Notices fade on their own, and the quiet × dismisses one.

Sync

Your usage history lives on your Mac and powers your trends. Turn on sync from the welcome sheet and that history (the same measures and verdicts, never any content) syncs to your own workspace, so it survives a restart and several Macs merge into one picture. Every batch that leaves your device is listed under "What has been shared", so you can see exactly what left and when.

The right-click menu

Right-click the pill for the rest: a live status line, what is being watched, Usage trends, What Drippy can see, Reset day and Quit.

Known limits of this test build

  • Apple silicon (M-series) Macs only.
  • Watches Anthropic traffic (Claude Desktop, Claude Code, browser Claude); other AI providers come later.
  • Not yet notarised, hence the first-launch step above.
  • Impact figures for network-observed apps are estimates with wide bands until finer sensing lands.
  • Spend is what the usage would cost at API list rates; a subscription bill will differ.

Feedback
