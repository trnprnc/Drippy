Privacy design

Drippy exists to make AI's hidden impacts visible. A tool like that works only if you can trust it more than the things it watches. This page describes what Drippy sees, what he keeps, and how those promises are enforced structurally in the code, not just by policy.

The two rules

  1. Verdicts, not content. Wherever Drippy touches text (clipboard, composer), the text is scanned in memory and discarded in the same function call. Only a category verdict survives, such as "API key" or "email address". There is no code path that stores, logs or transmits the value itself.
  2. Metadata before content. Drippy prefers the least-invasive signal that can do the job, and each deeper level is separately opt-in.

Your history, and where it goes

Your usage history lives on your Mac and powers the trends you see. Sync is a separate choice: turn it on, and your history (the same measures and verdicts, never any content) syncs to your own workspace, so it survives a restart and several Macs merge into one picture. Every batch that leaves your device is listed in the app under "What has been shared", so you can always see exactly what left and when.

Why the clipboard is watched during a session, not just when Claude is frontmost: in real work you copy a secret somewhere else (a browser, a .env file, a password manager) and then paste it into Claude. To warn you before that, Drippy checks the clipboard throughout an active Claude session, including a few minutes after you tab away to grab something. It never scans your clipboard when you have not been using Claude.

How loudly Drippy warns

Not everything sensitive is equally risky, and crying wolf over harmless things trains you to ignore the warnings that matter. So Drippy grades what he finds:

  • Tier 1, critical (full alarm and badge): credentials and secrets, payment details, government IDs, and identifiable personal data about other people (a patient's details, children's records, a list of personal records). These can cause real or irreversible harm, and de-identification in a training pipeline does not neutralise a live secret.
  • Tier 2, caution (violet, no badge): phone numbers, dates of birth, and a named person's HR details. Privacy-sensitive but low harm on their own.
  • Tier 3, low (noted, no warning): your own email address. You already gave Claude your email to use it; Drippy counts it for your trends but does not interrupt.

What Drippy stores on disk

  • ~/Library/Application Support/Drippy/: the pill's position and today's counters.
  • ~/Library/Application Support/Drippy/history/: your usage history (daily rollups and per-event records: timestamps, app names, token and energy drivers, privacy categories). Powers the Usage trends window, and syncs to your workspace only if you turn sync on.
  • ~/Library/Logs/Drippy.log: operational log with state changes, app names and privacy event categories. Never content.

Delete any of these at any time; Drippy recreates them empty.

The estimates

Impact numbers are derived from traffic volume (bytes ≈ tokens ≈ energy), never from reading your conversations, and are shown with their uncertainty (±3×). Claude Code numbers are exact, from its own local usage records.

Verifying these claims

Drippy's sensing surface is about 500 lines of readable source, and we intend to publish it so you do not have to take our word for any of this.
